This is going to be a shorter blog post. As I'm finalizing the blog addition to my portfolio website, Ilearned about typography line length and the benefit of limiting the horizontal width of text in articles like blogs. There are a few problems with wide horizontal text columns.

One problem is that the human eye can strain when moving long distances across the page horizontally. Our eyes find it easier to scan text across shorter distances. It also improves accessibility for people who lose their place in long lines.

The other problem is vertical tracking. A shorter line makes it easier to track your place in text vertically.

## What line length should I use?

To start, line length is best measured with characters. This is because measuring in inches or centimeters doesn't really take into account font point size, so the measure can completely change depending what your font configuration is. For this reason, most people use number of characters to measure line length.

[Practical typography](https://practicaltypography.com/) recommends a line length of **45-90 characters**. This is a good basepoint, but in web design it is also important to take accessibility into account. [w3.org](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/visual-presentation.html) recommends no more than 80 characters, so the sweet spot is about **45-80 characters**.

## Dealing with whitespace

Depending on your page layout, limiting your text columns to 80 characters can create whitespace. If you align the text with your left margin, this creates a larger whitespace on the right.

This isn't really a bad thing as it gives the reader some breathing room, however some devs may be bothered by an unbalanced page that has more white space on one side than the other. In this case, you can consider centering the text body and associated header as an alternative.

Another option is a multi-column layout, but this works better in traditional media like a newspaper than a website. Personally, I'm not a fan of this option.

In my case, I decided to keep my blog line length limited to 80 characters and aligned left with my margin, and I'm happy with how this came out.
